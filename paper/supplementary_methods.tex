\section{Switching Studies: Protocol and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:supp-switching}

These studies were added after reviewing the earlier non-switching CAGE
experiment. They are exploratory comparisons on fixed chronological or
constructed paths, rather than a new confirmatory held-out study.
Both exercise an observed budget crossing, but use different safe bases.
All methods receive the same path, act before its current observation,
and have the horizon announced in advance.

\subsection{NYC source and modeled allocation objective}
The source is NYC Open Data's Forestry Service Requests dataset,
\texttt{mu46-p9is}~\cite{nycforestry2026}. Frozen daily aggregates of registered
Hazard requests cover 1 January 2021 through 31 December 2022, with 365 days
per year and 26756 and 23670 requests, respectively. The reproduction archive
includes the recovered aggregate CSVs and provenance checksums.
The three coordinate groups are Brooklyn, Queens, and Bronx plus Manhattan
plus Staten Island. For counts $N_{t,j}$, set
$\ell_{t,j}=N_{t,j}/\sum_iN_{t,i}$. The single zero-count day uses uniform
shares after observation. No quantization or fitted regimes are used.

The learner chooses modeled unit resource shares $p_t\in\Delta_3$ before
observing that day's request shares. Counts are an administrative proxy;
staffing needs, workload intensity, costs, and service quality are not
measured. Each day has equal weight in this share-tracking game.
Let $P=L=\Delta_3$ and
\[
 u(p,\ell)=\frac{p-\ell}{\sqrt2},\qquad p^\star(\ell)=\ell.
\]
Every pure payoff $(e_i-e_j)/\sqrt2$ has norm at most one, hence the game is
normalized. Since $u(p^\star(z),z)=0$ for every $z$, the full strict target
is $S(Q)=\{0\}$ for every nonempty convex $Q$, including all interior
mixtures. The realized affine dimension is at most two.

\subsection{Game-specific safe certificate and causal controls}
A fresh $h$-round lag base plays $p_1=p_0=(1/3,1/3,1/3)$ and
$p_j=\ell_{j-1}$ for $j\ge2$. For every adaptive continuation,
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^h u(p_j,\ell_j)=\frac{p_0-\ell_h}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 h\operatorname{dist}(\bar u_h,S(Q_h))
 \le\frac{\operatorname{diam}(\Delta_3)}{\sqrt2}=1.
\]
Thus $B_0(0)=0$, $B_0(h)=1$ for $h>0$, and $G_T=1$ are certified before
observing data. This conservative diameter bound is sufficient; the tighter
$1/\sqrt3$ bound available for uniform $p_0$ is not used.
The base starts fresh, with a uniform first safe action and empty local lag
memory. It instantiates the abstract master with a game-specific base,
rather than the main paper's block-safety proposition.

The five methods are one-switch with this lag base, uninterrupted shared
past-hull, standalone lag-safe, window, and standalone original block-safe.
Window plays the mean of the previous at most $W=16$ observed shares and
also starts uniformly. No window or budget is tuned on these traces.
Block-safe retains the original block sizes, coordinate scaling, step sizes,
and certificate with learner dimension $k=2$. The master retains the original
fast update and observable residual trigger.
Its existing floating-point saddle LP and hull QP implementations check
numerical gaps; these diagnostics are numerical evidence, rather than
machine-verified exact-arithmetic certificates.

The primary replay is one continuous 730-day run. Separate fresh 365-day
runs over 2021 and 2022 check chronology-dependent behavior. They overlap
the combined trace and are not independent statistical trials.
No annual restart is inserted into the combined replay.
All methods use identical uniform initialization and post-choice monitoring.

\subsection{Metrics, complete traces, and crossings}
For each method $a$, retain actions, payoffs, prefix sums, mode, projection
and saddle diagnostics, residuals, and safe local indices.
The distinct metrics are
\[
 \delta_t^{(a)}=\left\|\frac1t\sum_{s=1}^t u_s^{(a)}\right\|_2,\qquad
 M_T^{(a)}=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\|u_t^{(a)}\|_2.
\]
The first is distance to the exact full strict target, allowing cancellation
of signed allocation errors. The second measures daily mismatch.
Cumulative differences are
\[
 D_b(t)=\sum_{s=1}^t
 \bigl(\|u_s^{(\mathrm{ours})}\|_2-\|u_s^{(b)}\|_2\bigr),
\]
where negative values favor the master on daily tracking. This is separate
from a target-distance difference or a native simulator cost.

On the combined trace, crossing occurs at $t=44$, 13 February 2021,
with $E_{44}=1.090736034998>1$. Round 44 remains fast; a fresh 686-round
lag base starts on round 45. The monitored residual then freezes.
The uninterrupted fast-only run supplies a separately labelled
counterfactual residual curve; uncalled fast residuals are not treated as
observed safe-mode quantities.
The annual 2021 replay also crosses at day 44, leaving 321 safe rounds;
the fresh 2022 replay crosses at day 26, leaving 339.
The combined run's logged projection-error estimates sum to about
$1.875\cdot10^{-7}$, and its additional saddle errors are zero.
These estimates are separate from the exact lag-base proof.

\subsection{Diagnostic with the original block routine and budget}
This test retains the original block-safe implementation and
$G_T=6\sqrt{k}T^{3/4}$. Take $P=[0,1]$ and the Euclidean subprobability
simplex $L=\operatorname{conv}\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_m\}\subset\mathbb R^m$.
Set $r(\ell)=\sum_i\ell_i\in[0,1]$ and
\[
 u(p,\ell)=p-r(\ell),\qquad p^\star(\ell)=r(\ell).
\]
The response prescribes capacity balancing. This scalar payoff is normalized
and biaffine; $S(Q)=\{0\}$ for every nonempty convex $Q$, and $k=1$.

For $T=16384$, 128 zero actions precede $e_1,\ldots,e_{16256}$, so $m=16256$.
The labels have identical aggregate unit demand. Their payoff-equivalent
identities deliberately remain in the fast opponent geometry; compressing
the entire game to $r(\ell)$ would remove the pathology.
This is an oracle/geometry diagnostic, rather than realistic attack learning,
fixed-game minimax difficulty, or a low-dimensional rate test.

Fast saddles and projections have closed forms. After zero is observed,
each new orthogonal basis vector projects to zero in the preceding hull,
giving unit residual. Scalar saddle maximization uses only the smallest
and largest previously observed aggregate demands. The zero-direction tie
uses the original uniform two-action mixture, i.e., capacity $1/2$.
With
\[
 G_T=6(16384)^{3/4}=8688.928127,
\]
crossing is at $128+\lfloor G_T\rfloor+1=8817$, with $E_{8817}=8689$.
The crossing round remains fast and a fresh original block routine runs
for 7567 rounds. Its lossless representation $(1-r,r)\in\Delta_2$ preserves
responses, gradients, block means, coordinate steps, and payoffs.
The fast projection retains the original subprobability-space geometry.

Every method starts at capacity $1/2$. Alongside fast-only and standalone
block-safe, lag plays the preceding demand and window its trailing 16-round
mean. Both use past aggregate demand, without an artificial restriction
to irrelevant labels.
Lag has its own stronger telescope $\sum_t(p_t-r_t)=p_1-r_T$; the tested
master retains the original block base.

\subsection{Reproduction and boundaries}
The script \texttt{scripts/run\_switching\_study.py} checks aggregate hashes,
reruns combined and annual NYC traces and the original-budget diagnostic,
and exports full NPZ trajectories, JSON summaries, software versions,
source checksums, and both English figures.
The window, budget, and scenarios are fixed; there is no configuration search.
The singleton target is evaluated analytically for every prefix.
Safe local indices, fresh-tail initialization, crossing flags, and masks
for uncalled fast residuals are retained.

Neither test evaluates the $q=4$ improvement. NYC uses real administrative
counts for a modeled tracking objective with a separately certified base.
The original-budget diagnostic exercises the original switch with exact
fast oracles but intentionally artificial high-dimensional labels.
Available author direction and AI technical assistance for this added
experimental design are recorded in the accompanying assistance ledger.

\subsection{Simulator, policies, and loss units}
We use the CAGE Challenge~2 simulator at revision
\texttt{26ce1c1253fa9e2e73f25e6a7f2da32860c11257}, with its original
\texttt{Scenario2.yaml} and reward implementation~\cite{kiely2023autonomous,cagechallenge2}.
The payoff banks are newly generated rollouts of this published open-source
benchmark. They are not a pre-existing published dataset of external network
outcomes. Each policy pair is evaluated in freshly initialized 50-step episodes.
The six defense policies are \texttt{monitor}, \texttt{react\_remove},
\texttt{react\_restore}, \texttt{decoy\_cycle}, \texttt{critical\_restore},
and \texttt{decoy\_react}. The first three use the supplied policies;
the other three are frozen scripted policies. The attack modes are
\texttt{sleep}, \texttt{meander}, and \texttt{b\_line}. Source code in the
supplement specifies every policy action and timing rule.
The listed policy and mode orders fix their indices throughout the experiment.

Native costs are positive counterparts of the simulator's negative rewards.
Their four components are host compromise, server compromise, operational
disruption, and restoration, including charged failed restore actions.
The sum is checked against the native reward at every simulator step.
Scenario-derived component bounds are $(0.8,4,10,1)$, whose Euclidean norm is
$10.84619749$. Dividing calibration mean vectors by this fixed constant
defines the normalized training tensor $u$; held-out outcomes do not change
the normalization, target response map, or policy menu. Native cost $J_m$
uses the unnormalized component sum. Vector distance $\delta_t$ is a separate
measure in the normalized training game.
Specifically, with the native per-step calibration mean vector $g_{ij}$ for policy $i$ and
mode $j$, $P=\Delta_6$, $L=\Delta_3$, $B=\|(0.8,4,10,1)\|_2$, and
\[
 u(p,\ell)=B^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^6\sum_{j=1}^3p_i\ell_jg_{ij}.
\]
Writing $\mathbf 1\in\mathbb R^4$ for the all-ones vector, the benchmark is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 i^\star(\ell)=\min\arg\min_{i\in[6]}
 \frac{1}{4B}\sum_{j=1}^3\ell_j\mathbf 1^\top g_{ij},\\
 p^\star(\ell)=e_{i^\star(\ell)}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
It minimizes the equally weighted mean of the four components, with the
lowest policy index resolving exact ties. This fixed response map determines
the full target $S(Q)$ used for all geometric diagnostics.

\subsection{Chronology and sampling units}
Calibration uses 40 seeds per cell, starting at 20261003. Validation uses
simulator seeds 38000000--38000099 and path seeds 40000000--40000019. The
held-out bank uses simulator seeds 39000000--39000399 and path seeds
41000000--41000049. Each seed table contains all 18 defense--attack cells;
seed indices are shared across cells, although policies can consume random
numbers differently. The two new banks contain 9000 simulator episodes
and 450000 native steps. Reusing a table across decision rounds does not
create additional independent simulator observations.

The curriculum uses phase fractions $(0.25,0.5,0.25)$ and initial Meander.
The broad phase reveals each known mode once, then samples modes uniformly.
The final phase samples from exponential weights mixed with 0.1 uniform
exploration. Its rewards are calibrated costs against a fixed uniform
reference defense, updated throughout the path. It learns mode selection
within a fixed library; it does not learn new native attack tactics or
react separately to each compared learner. All learner choices precede
revelation of the current sampled mode.

\subsection{Algorithms and parameter selection}
The scalar-aware saddle oracle minimizes predicted scalar cost subject to
the original saddle response constraints with allowance $\rho_{\mathrm{sc}}/\sqrt{t}$.
Here $\rho_{\mathrm{sc}}$ is stored as \texttt{rho}; it is separate from the
theoretical projection-gap notation.
It preserves the original dual saddle mixture and independently checks the
candidate's primal gap. An unsuccessful candidate returns the original
numerical saddle pair. The implementation uses floating-point checks with
oracle tolerance $10^{-9}$ and candidate-gap tolerance at most
$\max\{20\cdot10^{-9},10^{-8}\}$. These checks are numerical evidence.
The original one-switch threshold and safe routine are retained; with
$K=6$ policies, the horizon budget is $G_T=6\sqrt{K-1}T^{3/4}$.
The selected $\rho_{\mathrm{sc}}=0.25$ lies below one, so its saddle allowance
is within the nominal $t^{-1/2}$ contract rather than an additional
theoretical $\beta_t$ error. Floating-point certification remains a separate
implementation limitation.
Within each primary path, one learner is initialized once and retains its
state throughout all $T=512$ policy-selection rounds. Independent simulator
episode resets do not reset the learner. The forecast window $W=16$ is also
not a batch length or restart interval. No restart, safe switch, or
scalar-oracle fallback occurs in the primary selected-method test.
At $T=512$, $G_T=1444.07159$ exceeds the normalized residual upper bound
$2(T-1)=1022$, so a threshold crossing is impossible at this horizon.
Moreover, with three sampled pure modes, hull-expansion residuals occur only
when a previously unseen mode first appears; this protocol cannot exercise
continuing geometric novelty or demonstrate the benefit of safe switching.

The validation grid has $W\in\{4,8,16,32,64\}$ and
$\rho_{\mathrm{sc}}\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1\}$ for our oracle, the same five windows for window
best response, and Hedge rate multipliers in $\{0.25,0.5,1,2,4\}$.
Each family independently minimizes mean validation native cost, with exact
ties resolved by grid order. The selected configuration is serialized with
input and implementation checksums before accessing final-test outcomes.
Our method and Hedge initialize uniformly. Window initializes as a best
response to the known initial mode, then uses only past revealed modes.
Meander is the announced initial-mode convention; Window receives no future
mode path. Its first-action advantage reflects the differing initialization
rules. Window is a realizable online comparator.

The Hedge comparator uses full-information exponential weights, following
the classical method~\cite{freund1997hedge}; it receives the same calibrated
scalar loss table and post-choice mode information as the other methods.
For learning rates, let $L_{ij}=\mathbf 1^\top g_{ij}/(4B)$ and
$R=\max_{i,j}L_{ij}-\min_{i,j}L_{ij}$, computed from calibration.
Selected Hedge uses $\eta_H=4\sqrt{8\log(6)/T}/R$, while the attack-mode
selector uses $\eta_A=\sqrt{8\log(3)/T}/R$. Reporting $J_m$ uses the
unnormalized sum of native costs rather than this scalar training table.

\subsection{Separation from exploratory learner restarts}
Separate exploratory studies use $T=3000$ policy-selection rounds and
predeclared learner blocks of length $L=1000$. They compare clearing the
revealed-mode history with retaining its full global history. A full learner
restart resets the direction, accumulated residual budget, safe state, and
local clock; the calibration stays fixed. The first move of each new block
is uniform under the same initialization rule. Thus state restarts occur
at rounds 1001 and 2001. In the alternating scenario, attack modes change
every 500 rounds; this is distinct from the 1000-round learner restart.

These restarted variants are separate from the selected continuous method.
Their outcomes do not supply the primary $T=512$ cost table, confidence
intervals, or figures in this paper. No archived restart endpoint is imported
into those comparisons. A restarted learner would require a separately
stated segment-wise guarantee rather than inheriting the uninterrupted
master's global-horizon statement.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
The primary contrasts are ours minus window and ours minus Hedge. A crossed
percentile bootstrap independently resamples 400 whole simulator tables
and 50 whole paths, sharing the resulting weights across methods. It uses
10000 draws with seed 44000000 and Bonferroni coverage for two contrasts.
The resulting approximate simultaneous intervals condition on the fixed
calibration and selected configuration; calibration and selection uncertainty
are excluded. No separate round or policy cell is treated as an independent
resampling unit.

Descriptive dynamics and component figures use 2000 crossed draws, seed
46000000, with pointwise 95\% intervals. They were added after the primary
study and do not replace its predeclared contrasts. Dynamics plots use a
trailing 16-round average solely for display. Cumulative differences use
the factor 50 for expected total cost of separately reset episodes.
Path distributions show all 50 path-averaged paired differences conditional
on the same held-out bank. Validation sensitivity uses the entire original
20-setting oracle grid and introduces no new parameter selection.

\subsection{Full-target numerical geometry}
We compute distance to the full closed convex response target $S(Q_t)$,
including all response cells over interior hull mixtures. Projection tolerance
is $10^{-11}$; lower and upper distances, squared objective gap, its square
root, feasibility residual, success, and hull dimension are retained.
All 50 primary paths are evaluated at 39 shared checkpoints, including
powers of two, every 16th round, phase boundaries, and first mode appearances.
Both moving $S(Q_t)$ and retrospective fixed $S(Q_T)$ distances are computed;
the fixed target is used only for evaluation. The target expands when new
modes first appear, which can itself reduce the distance.

A separately declared descriptive sweep uses all path seeds
45000000--45000019 at each horizon $64,128,256,512,1024,2048$, producing
120 common paths and 360 method trajectories. These are separate announced
horizon runs. Curriculum phase lengths and original learner/opponent
learning-rate formulas scale with the horizon; selected parameters stay fixed.
No new simulator episodes are collected. The median one-switch distance at
$T=64$ is $1.87074\cdot10^{-4}$, with 5 of 20 paths at the numerical display
floor. At every $T\ge128$, all one-switch and window distances are near
machine precision. Hedge medians decline from 0.0153491 to 0.00211179.
The one-switch and window decay slopes are unidentified. Any descriptive
Hedge slope concerns this horizon-scaled experiment family.

Values at or below $10^{-10}$ are counted separately in the figures, without
epsilon substitution for logarithms. Quantile bands whose lower boundary
reaches that display threshold are omitted from the log panel. Numerical
uncertainty from projection gaps is distinct from path variability.
Only realized affine dimension at most two is available in this three-mode
game, so these checks cannot evaluate the improved $q=4$ exponent.

\subsection{Reproduction and limits}
The accompanying README specifies installation, cached-bank smoke tests,
figure reconstruction, and full simulator recollection. Source hashes,
software versions, all selected trajectories, complete banks, and locked
configurations are supplied. A source ledger preserves its original line
endings and a documented serialization repair; no scientific arrays or
frozen algorithm bytes were changed. The anonymous presentation removes
only public archive-link metadata from the primary analysis; both source
and derivative checksums are recorded and every numerical field is retained.
An account of author direction and AI technical assistance is supplied in
\texttt{supplement/experiments/AI\_ASSISTANCE.md} in this project, with available prompts and
record limitations.

This study concerns calibrated full-information policy selection with reset
episodes and a fixed attacker library. It does not test persistent deployed
networks, new attack tactics, an advantage from activating the safe fallback,
or the empirical performance of Marinov et al.'s algorithms. Smaller native
cost relative to Hedge does not establish superiority over window or prove
the vector convergence theorem.
