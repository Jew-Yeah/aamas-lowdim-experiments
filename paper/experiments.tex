\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:cage-experiments}

\paragraph{Setting and scope.}
We evaluate policy selection in the CAGE Challenge~2 simulator~\cite{kiely2023autonomous},
using six fixed defense policies, three known attack modes, and four native
cost components: host compromise, server compromise, operational disruption,
and restoration. Each decision round selects a distribution over complete
defense policies for a separately reset 50-step episode; mixture outcomes are
evaluated in expectation. The finite payoff model is calibrated beforehand,
and the current attack mode is disclosed after the defense choice.
For $T=512$, a common attacker curriculum uses Meander for rounds 1--128,
explores the three modes in rounds 129--384, and then uses an exponential-weights
mode selector trained against a fixed uniform reference defense. Thus the
compared methods face the same external paths.

\paragraph{Methods and validation.}
Our implementation retains the one-switch master and its safe budget, and
uses a scalar forecast to choose among numerically admissible saddle responses.
The forecast averages the last $W$ revealed modes; the saddle-error allowance
is $\rho_{\mathrm{sc}}/\sqrt{t}$. The scalar allowance parameter
$\rho_{\mathrm{sc}}$ (serialized as \texttt{rho}) is distinct from the
projection-gap notation in the theory. Separate validation selects $W=16$
and $\rho_{\mathrm{sc}}=0.25$,
a window best response with $W=16$, and Hedge with learning-rate multiplier~4.
Our method and Hedge start uniformly; window responds to the known initial
Meander mode. Calibration uses 40 simulator seeds per policy pair.
Validation uses 100 seed tables and 20 common paths; the final test uses
400 new seed tables and 50 new common paths. Parameters are frozen before
final-test evaluation.

\paragraph{Metrics and uncertainty.}
Let $c_t^{(m)}$ be method $m$'s native per-step cost at round $t$, averaged
over the shared test tables and paths, and let
$J_m=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}c_t^{(m)}$. The native cost is the sum of the four
components, with smaller values preferred. For comparator $b$, the paired
difference is $\Delta J_b=J_{\mathrm{ours}}-J_b$; negative values favor our
method. The two predeclared contrasts use 10,000 crossed bootstrap draws of
whole simulator seed tables and whole paths, retaining pairing across methods.
Bonferroni adjustment gives approximate simultaneous 95\% intervals,
conditional on the fixed calibration and selected parameters.

\paragraph{Results and interpretation.}
Our native cost is 5.3\% lower than tuned Hedge and 0.24\% higher than window
(Table~\ref{tab:cage-primary}). Every test path has identical one-switch and
window actions after round one; the entire window deficit comes from the
different initialization, explaining the plateau in
Figure~\ref{fig:cage-cumulative}. No safe switch occurs on these paths.
Separate descriptive full-target checks compute
$\delta_t=\operatorname{dist}(\bar u_t,S(Q_t))$ in the normalized calibration
game, including responses to all mixtures in the realized hull.
With three attack modes, $q_T\le2$. In 20 new paths at each announced horizon
$T\in\{64,128,256,512,1024,2048\}$, all one-switch and window terminal
distances are at numerical precision for $T\ge128$; their empirical decay
exponents cannot be estimated. These experiments illustrate behavior in a
calibrated policy game; they do not test the $q=4$ improvement, establish a
benefit of switching, or compare with the algorithms of Marinov et al.
The anonymous supplement supplies configurations, seeds, software versions,
source checksums, additional figures, replay instructions, and a description
of AI assistance in experimental design and implementation.
